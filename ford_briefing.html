Own money changes decisions. A recall is a required safety fix Ford pays for. A tariff is an import tax Ford pays. A write-off cuts reported profit. All three problems follow records Ford already has: repairs, vehicle data, supplier updates, real cost per vehicle. The fix is speed, not new factories.

Problem 01 · Quality

152 recalls in 2025 — the most any carmaker has ever had

~40
Were repeat fixes

The repeats: software updates sent to vehicles but never confirmed installed. Source: CBT News

Do now

Freeze launches that fail quality gates

No overrides. Late trucks beat recalled trucks.

This quarter

Pay dealers $500 for first defect reports

Cheapest early warning Ford can buy.

Big call

Bill suppliers for repeat defect costs

Quality becomes their money problem too.

Crazy

Cut the options list in half

Fewer parts, fewer ways to fail.

Problem 02 · Supply

One missing part stopped the F-150 for a week

~$1.5B
Aluminum fire cost

Dearborn and Kansas City plants were idle in September 2026, after the 2025 aluminum supplier fires. Sources: Yahoo Finance · Detroit Free Press

Do now

Stockpile the 20 line-stopping parts

Cash on shelves beats a stopped plant.

This quarter

Ban single sources on critical parts

Exceptions need the CFO's signature.

Big call

Prepay suppliers — or buy a stake

Upfront money buys priority when parts run short.

Crazy

Fund a second aluminum plant

If one fire can stop the F-150, own the backup.

Problem 03 · EVs

Ford lost $4.8B on EVs — and wrote off $19.5B

2029
EV break-even target

Tariffs added ~$2B in 2025, including a $900M retroactive bill. Source: NY Post / Fox Business

Do now

Kill EV models that miss profit in 12 months

Lightning was already cut. Finish the job.

This quarter

License the EV platform — stop building

Save the capex for hybrids and trucks.

Big call

Tie EV bonuses to profit per vehicle

What gets paid, gets done.

Crazy

Sell or spin off the EV unit

Keep software, Pro and the brand.

At the booth

Two lines to remember

Say

"All three problems are one problem: Ford learns too late. The fixes start with data Ford already has."

Ask

"How does Ford find a defect before NHTSA does?"

"How far past tier-1 suppliers can Ford actually see?"
