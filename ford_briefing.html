The three headlines look different — recalls, supply shocks, EV losses — but they share one cause: Ford finds out about problems too late to fix them cheaply. This briefing explains each problem in plain words, then shows four levels of fixes for each: something Ford can start now, something to build next, a bigger bet, and a long-term idea. Nothing here needs a new factory — it starts with information Ford already has.

Problem 01

Too many safety recalls

152 recalls
In 2025 · The most ever
In one sentence: Ford had to bring vehicles back to dealers for safety fixes 152 times in 2025 — the most of any carmaker in a single year — and about 40 of those were repeat notices for software fixes that were sent out but never confirmed as installed.

A recall means the carmaker calls vehicles back to a dealership for a safety repair, paid for by Ford. Ford has doubled its safety team and says warranty costs are easing, but the failures still fill dealer repair bays and leave some new vehicles stuck at factories. CBT News

Start now · within 90 days

One weekly quality page

Put warranty claims, dealer repair notes and vehicle alerts on a single page. Every red flag gets a name and a deadline.

Why it helps: everyone works from the same facts — problems get caught in weeks, not quarters.
What could go wrong: dealer notes arrive late and messy; start rough and improve.
Build next · 3-9 months

Automatic early-warning alerts

Flag when the same repair shows up too often by plant, model or parts batch — and confirm every software fix actually installed.

Why it helps: catches defects before they become recalls; could have prevented the ~40 repeats.
What could go wrong: false alarms; tune before trusting them.
Bigger bet · 9-24 months

A health score for every Ford

Like a credit score for a car: a 1-100 score that tells owners and dealers to fix an issue before it fails. Customers opt in.

Why it helps: fewer breakdowns, and customers come to Ford for the fix.
What could go wrong: privacy questions; pilot with one model first.
Long-term idea · 2+ years

Catch defects before the factory

A computer model of the whole fleet tests design changes and parts batches before they are built. Ford pays technicians and owners for early defect reports.

Why it helps: recalls become rare — quality turns back into a selling point.
What could go wrong: big culture change; needs careful legal review.
Watch these numbers: recalls per year · repeat recalls · warranty cost per vehicle · days from first sign to action · share of software fixes confirmed installed
Problem 02

One supplier can stop the F-150

~1 week
F-150 plants idle · Sept 2026
In one sentence: One missing part shut down the factories that build the F-150 — America's best-selling truck — for about a week in September 2026, and with the 2025 fires at an aluminum supplier, the total bill is expected to reach about $1.5B.

The shutdown hit the Dearborn and Kansas City plants. The aluminum fires cost about $800M in the first half of 2025 alone. Yahoo Finance · Detroit Free Press

Start now · within 90 days

Supplier risk map

Score the top 200 parts for risk: only one supplier? financially healthy? in a fire or flood zone? how many days of stock on hand?

Why it helps: the 20 risks most likely to stop a line become visible.
What could go wrong: Ford can't see past its direct suppliers — mark unknowns as risky.
Build next · 3-9 months

Control tower + practice drills

Track supplier, port and weather news with a playbook attached: who calls whom, where else to buy. Practice losing a top supplier before it happens.

Why it helps: downtime measured in days becomes hours; rush shipping costs fall.
What could go wrong: too many alerts kill trust — start with 50 parts.
Bigger bet · 9-24 months

Extra stock as insurance

For critical parts, pay a small premium — extra stock or a second supplier — when the cost of a stoppage is clearly bigger.

Why it helps: trade a small carrying cost for avoiding hundred-million-dollar losses.
What could go wrong: competes with other budgets; plants have physical limits.
Long-term idea · 2+ years

Supply-shock insurance

Treat each critical part like an insurance policy: price the risk, then fund extra stock or small local backup production (like 3D printing).

Why it helps: Ford can promise trucks on time even in bad years.
What could go wrong: complex to run; needs suppliers to share data.
Watch these numbers: plant downtime hours · rush shipping spend · share of critical parts with a backup · days of stock on hand · estimated recovery time per supplier
Problem 03

Losing money on electric vehicles

$11.1B
Q4 2025 loss · largest since 2008
In one sentence: Ford lost $4.8B on electric vehicles in 2025 and wrote off $19.5B of EV investments — so it is rethinking where to spend — while import taxes (tariffs) cost about $2B in 2025 and are expected to repeat in 2026.

A write-off is an accounting way of admitting an investment is now worth less; it hits reported profit. Ford now expects $4B-$4.5B more EV losses in 2026 and aims for EV break-even in 2029. NY Post / Fox Business

Start now · within 90 days

Know what each vehicle really earns

One monthly page per model and plant: selling price minus parts, tariffs, batteries, warranty and discounts = real profit per vehicle.

Why it helps: keep / fix / stop decisions get made on facts instead of hunches.
What could go wrong: cost data sits in many systems — first drafts will be rough.
Build next · 3-9 months

A "what if" planning room

Rehearse scenarios: if tariffs rise, if tax credits change, if gas prices move — what does Ford build, where, and what does it stop? Rules agreed in advance.

Why it helps: the response is ready before the news arrives.
What could go wrong: if finance doesn't own the assumptions, it becomes a debating club.
Bigger bet · 9-24 months

Lean into hybrids and paid software

Invest where the money is today — hybrids, trucks, commercial vans — limit EV spending until costs fall, and grow paid features (driver assistance, fleet tools) as repeating revenue.

Why it helps: less reliance on one-time vehicle sales.
What could go wrong: software must be priced credibly; new skills needed.
Long-term idea · 2+ years

Sell energy and uptime, not just trucks

Parked electric trucks can sell power back to the grid (vehicle-to-grid). Batteries get a second life. Fleets pay a subscription for trucks that stay on the road.

Why it helps: new revenue that doesn't depend on selling more vehicles.
What could go wrong: utility rules; upfront investment needed.
Watch these numbers: profit per vehicle · EV loss per vehicle · tariff cost per vehicle · mix of hybrid, electric and gas vs plan · software revenue per vehicle

Plain-English glossary

Words used in this page

Recall
When a carmaker calls vehicles back to a dealer to fix a safety problem — paid for by the carmaker, not the owner.
Warranty
Ford's promise to pay for certain repairs during a set period. High warranty costs mean more things are breaking.
Tariff
A tax a government charges on imported goods. The company importing — Ford — pays it, which eats into profit.
Write-off
An accounting step that admits an investment is now worth less than expected. It reduces reported profit but is not cash leaving the bank that day.
Software fix sent over the air
A repair delivered like a phone update, without a dealer visit — but Ford must be able to confirm it actually installed.
Direct (tier-1) supplier
A company that ships parts straight to Ford. The suppliers behind them — tier 2 and tier 3 — are often invisible, which hides risk.
